## What you will learn

- Aggregate with `COUNT`, `SUM`, `AVG`, `MIN` and `MAX`, and know what each ignores
- Group by one or several columns and explain the resulting row count
- Filter on an aggregate with `HAVING`, and know why `WHERE` cannot do it
- Sort with `NULLS FIRST` / `NULLS LAST` and by multiple keys

## Why it matters

Reporting questions — "how many orders per customer last month" — are the bulk of day-to-day SQL work, and they are exactly where `WHERE` versus `HAVING` trips people up.

## A worked example

```sql
SELECT
    customer_id,
    COUNT(*)                          AS order_count,
    SUM(total_cents)                  AS revenue_cents,
    AVG(total_cents)                  AS avg_order_cents
FROM orders
WHERE placed_at >= DATE '2026-01-01'
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING COUNT(*) > 3
ORDER BY revenue_cents DESC
NULLS LAST;
```

`COUNT(*)` counts rows. `COUNT(column)` counts rows where that column is not null — a difference that quietly changes totals.

<Callout type="interview" title="Interview corner">
**What is the "grain" of a query result?**

The grain is what one row of the output represents. After `GROUP BY customer_id`, one row represents one customer. Every selected column must be valid at that grain — which is why you cannot select a raw `order_id` alongside a `GROUP BY customer_id` without aggregating it.
</Callout>

## Quick recap

- Know the grain of your result before adding columns.
- `WHERE` filters rows before grouping; `HAVING` filters after.
- `COUNT(*)` counts rows, `COUNT(col)` counts non-null values.
