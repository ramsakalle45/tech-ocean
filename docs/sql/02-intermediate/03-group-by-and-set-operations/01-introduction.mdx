## What you will learn

- Group by several columns and by expressions
- Use `GROUPING SETS`, `ROLLUP` and `CUBE` for subtotals
- Combine result sets with `UNION`, `INTERSECT` and `EXCEPT`
- Know that `UNION` deduplicates and `UNION ALL` does not

## Why it matters

Reporting often needs subtotals at several levels, and a pivot table of categories is much easier with `ROLLUP` than with a chain of joins.

## A worked example

```sql
SELECT
    region,
    category,
    SUM(revenue_cents) AS revenue_cents,
    GROUPING(category) AS category_is_total
FROM sales
GROUP BY ROLLUP (region, category)
ORDER BY region, category_is_total, category;
```

`GROUPING()` tells you a value was produced by the rollup rather than by the data — that is how you tell a real category from a subtotal row.

<Callout type="interview" title="Interview corner">
**When would you use `UNION ALL` instead of `UNION`?**

When you know the two sets are disjoint, or when duplicates are meaningful. `UNION` has to sort or hash both sides to deduplicate, which is a real cost on large inputs. `UNION ALL` just concatenates. It is also the right choice when you are appending event logs that may legitimately repeat.
</Callout>

## Quick recap

- `UNION` removes duplicates and sorts; `UNION ALL` does neither.
- `ROLLUP` gives hierarchical subtotals; `CUBE` gives every combination.
- Use `GROUPING()` to mark subtotal rows.
