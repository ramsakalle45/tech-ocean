## What you will learn

- Compare fan-out on write, fan-out on read, and hybrid approaches
- Choose between a pull model and a push model and justify it
- Design a ranking layer that can be changed without a redeploy
- Handle the "celebrity" case where fan-out explodes

## Why it matters

The feed is the canonical trade-off between write cost and read latency, and it forces you to reason about a data distribution that is extremely skewed.

## A worked example

```text
Fan-out on write (push):
  post → write to every follower's feed list
  + read is a single list read: fast, ~O(1) latency
  - write cost is O(followers) — one post from a celebrity is huge
  - feed lists go stale the moment a follow changes

Fan-out on read (pull):
  read → fetch followees' recent posts, merge and rank
  + write is always O(1)
  - read is slow and gets slower with more followees
  - the classic "followed 5000 people" problem

Hybrid:
  push for normal accounts, pull for high-follower accounts
  at read time, merge the two

Ranking:
  candidates (recent posts from followees) → score → top N
  Keep scoring in a separate service so it can change daily
  without a feed-service deploy.
```

Most real feeds are hybrid, and the reason is the follower distribution, not the average case.

<Callout type="interview" title="Interview corner">
**A celebrity with 10 million followers posts an update. How do you avoid writing 10 million feed entries?**

Do not push to their followers. Mark the account as high-follower, and at read time fetch that account’s posts separately and merge them into the ranked result. The general version of this is the hybrid model: push for most accounts, pull for the tail, and merge at read. The cost moves from write time to read time, and it only affects the small number of people who follow a celebrity.
</Callout>

## Quick recap

- Push is fast to read and expensive to write; pull is the reverse.
- The follower distribution decides the model, not the average case.
- Use cursor pagination — offsets break on a changing feed.
