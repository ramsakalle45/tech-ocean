## What you will learn

- Run a 45-minute design interview with a clear structure
- Ask the clarifying questions that actually change the design
- Estimate scale, and state your assumptions out loud
- Present a design with its trade-offs stated rather than implied

## Why it matters

Most candidates lose design interviews on process, not knowledge. A fixed structure frees you to think about the actual problem.

## A worked example

```text
1. Requirements       (5 min)
   Who uses it? What are the core features? What must be fast
   and what can be slow?

2. Scale              (5 min)
   How many users? How many requests/second? How much data?
   Write the numbers down. State your assumptions.

3. API and data model (5 min)
   The endpoints, and the shape of the main entities.
   This is where most designs go wrong — sketching the system
   before knowing what it stores.

4. High-level design  (10 min)
   The boxes and the arrows. Say the direction of each arrow
   and what crosses it.

5. Deep dive         (15 min)
   Pick the two components that matter most. Usually the
   write path and the read path.

6. Wrap up           (5 min)
   Failure modes, monitoring, and what you would do with more
   time.

Always: talk through your thinking. A silent candidate is
impossible to help, and silence looks like guessing.
```

If the interviewer says "you can go one level deeper", they are telling you which part to expand. Take the hint.

<Callout type="interview" title="Interview corner">
**How do you start a design interview?**

With questions, not a diagram. What is being built, for whom, and what is the defining constraint — read-heavy or write-heavy, huge payloads, real-time, or batch? Then the scale numbers, out loud, so we can correct each other if my assumptions are wrong. I want to know what matters before I draw anything, because the same problem has very different designs at a hundred requests per second and a million.
</Callout>

## Quick recap

- Questions first, then numbers, then the diagram.
- Name the read and write paths before anything else.
- State the trade-off you are making, out loud.
